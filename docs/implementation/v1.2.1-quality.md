# River v1.2.1 document quality

Status: hosted comparison review in progress. Sixteen of eighteen exports have been collected and verified; all twenty-two exported pages and four reference pages have been visually reviewed. Production is outside scope.

## Method

Three datasets use frozen wording and section order: a separate résumé made from the owner's existing saved facts, fictional one-page Maya Chen, and fictional two-page Jordan Alvarez. Independent references were composed in ReportLab. Each comparison was assembled through River's normal signed-in workflow and exported from an immutable saved version.

The collector checks the recorded export, theme, every nested entry layout, renderer validation, exact intended text/order, PDF/report hashes, and expected page count. The page audit checks nonempty pages, glyph bounds, link annotations, and at least 10 TeX points of body text. PDF points are converted using 72.27/72. Every inspected page is retained beside its reference.

## Findings so far

- Maya: all six theme/layout combinations passed. The name and section headings establish a clear hierarchy. Contact links appear once, dates align, and bullets remain readable. Compact combines employer/title and reduces entry spacing without dropping content.
- Jordan: all six theme/layout combinations passed on both pages. Minimal/Classic places Projects at the start of page two, closest to the independent reference. Other layouts can continue Projects at a complete bullet boundary; headings remain with their first bullet. Some second pages have more open space than the reference but remain coherent, readable continuations.
- Personal: Classic and Minimal with both entry layouts passed. Long employer/title combinations wrap within the heading column without colliding with dates. The saved legacy whole date range remains intact. All five sections remain present. Both Technical combinations remain outstanding.
- Across the twenty-two inspected hosted pages: no clipping, overlap, accidental blank page, stranded heading, or undersized body text. The minimum body text is 10 TeX points and the glyph-bound audit found zero out-of-bounds glyphs. River is denser than the independently designed reference, with comparable readable hierarchy and alignment.
